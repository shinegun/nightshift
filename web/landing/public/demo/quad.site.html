Wanting to study with someone isn't the hard part

In one course-level study, 78 percent of students joined at least one group study session in a semester, a median of four sessions. Asked why they turned up, they said a professor encouraged it or a classmate invited them.

So the problem is the invitation. Study groups happen when somebody asks, and asking depends on luck: sitting next to the right person, mentioning that you're free Thursday. In an intro lecture of 200 people, that luck runs out fast.

Loneliness is measured and widespread. Fifty-two percent of students reported high loneliness in the 2024-25 Healthy Minds Study. Quad isn't a fix for that. It's the smaller thing that's easier to arrange: an hour on Tuesday when someone in your course expects you in a library room.

The tools that exist solve half the problem. Discord servers, GroupMe threads and class chats are good at finding people in your course. None of them will tell you who's free Tuesday at two and turn that into a standing meeting. That last step is Quad.

Sources: Faculty Focus on group study participation, Healthy Minds Study 2024-25 on loneliness.

What Quad does

  • Matched on two things

    Your courses, and the hours you're usually free. No personality quiz, no popularity, no feed of who's online right now.

  • Three to five people, one standing time

    Your group gets a recurring slot that works for everyone, plus a suggested spot: a library room or a cafe near campus.

  • Quiet by design

    A group chat for rescheduling, and a way to leave a group that isn't working. Nothing to scroll, no follower counts.

How it works

  1. Sign in with your university email

    Verification only. Quad doesn't connect to your school's student records or course system.

  2. Add this term's courses

    Course names are enough. No grades, no assignments, no transcripts.

  3. Mark the hours you're usually free

    Tuesday and Thursday afternoons, or the gap between two lectures.

  4. Get your group

    Once enough classmates in the same course overlap with you, three to five of you land in a group with a time and a place.

Matching takes more than one person. If your course doesn't have enough Quad students yet, we'll tell you that instead of dropping you into a group that never meets.

Questions we get asked

Which campus are you starting with?

One big public university with intro lectures of a few hundred students, because that's where there are enough classmates to match. We're confirming it now and will name it once it's settled. Join the waitlist and we'll email you if it's yours.

What does Quad know about me?

Your name, your university email, your school, the courses you're taking, and the hours you're free. That's the whole list. Quad doesn't read your student records, and you can delete your account whenever you want.

How many people end up in a group?

Three to five, all in the same course. Small enough that everyone talks, big enough to survive one person getting sick.

What if nobody in my course is free when I am?

Then there's no group for you yet, and we'll say so. We'd rather tell you that than put you in something that quietly stops meeting. You'll see how many classmates in your course are on Quad, and we'll email you when the overlap is there.

Where do groups meet?

Public places: library rooms, cafes near campus. Quad never suggests a home address, and you can report or block anyone in your group.

Does it cost anything?

Being matched into a group is free. If we ever add paid extras, they'd be things a group organizer wants, like a private room, and we'd say so before charging anyone.

Do I have to be friends with my group?

No. Plenty of people want a study group and not a friend group. Show up, work for two hours, go home.
